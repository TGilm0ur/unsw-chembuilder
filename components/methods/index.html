Approximations used in ChemBuilder

Real quantum chemistry programs solve for the electrons in a molecule. ChemBuilder replaces each step with a simple classical model. Use this page when you compare your results with experimental or higher-accuracy data.

The methods in a little more detail

Energy minimisation. Bonds, angles and twists behave like springs, and charged atoms attract or repel. The atoms are moved step by step until the total energy stops falling. The animation shows these real steps. This is usually a starting point for a quantum optimisation, not a replacement for it.

Partial charges (Gasteiger). Charge is shifted along each bond towards the more electronegative atom, and this is repeated until the electronegativities balance. No electron density is calculated.

Dipole moment. Each atom's charge is multiplied by its position and the results are added as vectors. The size of the result is the dipole moment, in debye (D).

IR spectrum. Each bond stretches like a spring. The wavenumber depends on the spring strength (a typical value for that type of bond) and the reduced mass of the two atoms. Peak strength grows with bond polarity. Bends and twists are left out.

Enthalpy. (enthalpy of formation) is the energy change when one mole of the molecule is made from its elements. ChemBuilder uses Hess's Law to find it. First atomise the elements (this costs energy), then form the molecule's bonds (this releases energy) using average bond enthalpies.

Entropy and free energy. The molecule's entropy is estimated from its mass (translation), shape and symmetry (rotation), and its bond vibrations. The entropy change on forming the molecule from its elements, , needs the standard entropies of the elements. Assuming and do not change with temperature, the free energy of formation is negative (spontaneous) when the energy released outweighs the entropy term.
